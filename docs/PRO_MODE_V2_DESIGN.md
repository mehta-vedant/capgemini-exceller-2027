# Exceller Pro Mode v2 — learning and interface direction

## What changed after the product audit

The first Pro implementation gave every day a title, three short explanatory blocks, a visual, and links into practice. That made the roadmap complete on paper but did not make a single day feel like a complete tutoring session. The v2 design treats each day as a deliberate study session rather than an article.

## Learning loop

Each day follows this sequence:

1. **Mission** — the decision or skill to leave with.
2. **Guided case** — an engineering situation, decomposed step by step.
3. **Decision checkpoint** — choose an approach before being shown the rationale.
4. **Active recall** — short retrieval prompts with feedback.
5. **Graded scenario** — answer an original scenario question in the lesson itself.
6. **Build task** — create, trace, debug, explain, or review something tangible.
7. **Reflection and revision** — identify the transfer rule to keep.

This sequence responds to candidate reports of long, applied MCQs: the learner rehearses reading constraints, choosing trade-offs, and explaining why alternatives fail—not only recognising a definition. It also follows evidence-informed instructional principles: use a worked example before independent transfer, reduce irrelevant visual load, and place retrieval with immediate feedback alongside instruction.

## Visual direction

The previous lime-on-dark system was too uniform and made a large learning page feel like one dense dashboard. The redesigned system uses a quieter ink-and-paper academy surface: a warm off-white reading canvas, deep evergreen navigation, slate text, and amber only for decisions or alerts. Colour has a job: navigation, module identity, completion, or warning. The lesson page alternates full-width reading sections with compact interactive cards, helping long sessions breathe.

## Behaviour rules

- A lesson completion is earned through instructional blocks, not via a generic link.
- An in-lesson scenario creates a real saved attempt and immediately explains all choices.
- Active recall and daily reflection persist locally, but no readiness metric is fabricated from them.
- The day can be completed only after its reading blocks and its applied checkpoint are complete.
- Existing Lab routes remain untouched; Pro links into them for deeper practice.

## Evidence boundaries

Repeated candidate reports support applied AI/technical MCQs, debugging, AI-assisted coding, and cognitive/behavioural modules as preparation priorities. They do not establish a campus-specific sequence, cutoff, compensation allocation, or an expansion of the unresolved term “RRP”.
